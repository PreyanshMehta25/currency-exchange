# FX Insight — Research & Econometric Methodology

## 1. Project Background and Research Objective

Foreign exchange (FX) rates represent the relative valuation between sovereign currencies, dictated by international capital flows, monetary policy divergence, inflation expectations, trade balances, and geopolitical events. For importers, exporters, multinational corporate treasuries, and quantitative portfolio managers, currency movements introduce substantial volatility and cash-flow risk.

### Core Research Objective
> "To comparatively evaluate statistical and machine-learning-based forecasting techniques for predicting exchange-rate movements across multiple currency pairs and forecast horizons, identifying the models that provide the most accurate and consistent forecasts."

### Core Research Questions
1. **Comparative Accuracy:** Which forecasting model performs best across diverse currency pairs (e.g. major pairs like EUR/USD vs emerging cross-rates like USD/INR)?
2. **Horizon Decay:** Does forecasting error (MAE, RMSE, MAPE) degrade non-linearly as the forecast horizon expands from $H=7$ to $H=15$ and $H=30$ observation business days?
3. **Statistical vs Machine Learning Paradigms:** Do nonlinear ensemble architectures (Random Forest Regressors) outperform classical linear stochastic models (ARIMA, Holt's Exponential Smoothing)?
4. **Economic Efficiency vs Complexity:** Does any model generate statistically significant outperformance over the Naive Last-Observation Random Walk benchmark?

## 2. Time-Series Econometric Diagnostics

### 2.1 Stationarity Testing Framework
Financial asset prices typically exhibit non-stationarity in levels. To avoid spurious regression, two complementary diagnostic tests are implemented:

#### 1. Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) Test
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** The series possesses a unit root (Non-stationary).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$):** The series was generated by a stationary process.
- **Specification:**
  $$\Delta y_t = \alpha + \beta t + \gamma y_{t-1} + \sum_{i=1}^p \delta_i \Delta y_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t$$
- **Interpretation:** If $p < 0.05$, reject $H_0$; the series is stationary. If $p \ge 0.05$, fail to reject $H_0$; the series exhibits a unit root.

#### 2. Kwiatkowski-Phillips-Schmidt-Shin (KPSS) Test
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** The series is level stationary (Stationary).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$):** The series possesses a unit root (Non-stationary).
- **Interpretation:** If $p \ge 0.05$, fail to reject $H_0$ (stationarity confirmed). If $p < 0.05$, reject $H_0$.

#### Dual Confirmation Matrix
- Levels: ADF $p > 0.05$ and KPSS $p < 0.05$ $\implies$ Raw exchange rates are non-stationary with a unit root.
- First Differences ($\Delta y_t = y_t - y_{t-1}$): ADF $p < 0.01$ and KPSS $p > 0.10$ $\implies$ First differences are stationary.
- **Conclusion:** Foreign exchange rate series are confirmed to be Integrated of Order 1, $I(1)$.

### 2.2 Time-Series Decomposition
Additive decomposition models the observed series as:
$$y_t = T_t + S_t + R_t$$
where $T_t$ is trend, $S_t$ is the seasonal component at candidate period $s$, and $R_t$ is the residual shock.

**Academic Reality on Currency Seasonality:** Unlike agricultural commodity prices or energy demand, daily foreign exchange rates do not exhibit strong deterministic calendar seasonality. Instead, they adhere closely to martingale dynamics. The platform calculates the *Seasonal Variance Ratio*:
$$\text{Ratio} = \frac{\text{Var}(S_t)}{\text{Var}(y_t - T_t)}$$
If this ratio is below 10%, the platform explicitly advises the user that seasonal decomposition is exploratory and does not reflect a physical economic cycle.

## 3. Implemented Forecasting Architectures

### Model 1: Autoregressive Linear Regression
- **Feature Set:** Lag 1, Lag 2, Lag 3, Lag 5, Lag 10, Rolling Mean (5, 10 obs), Rolling Std (5 obs).
- **Specification:**
  $$\hat{y}_{t+1} = \beta_0 + \sum_{k \in \{1,2,3,5,10\}} \beta_k y_{t-k+1} + \gamma_1 \bar{y}_{t, 5} + \gamma_2 \bar{y}_{t, 10} + \theta s_{t, 5}$$
- **Multi-Step Strategy:** Pure recursive projection. At step $h$, predicted value $\hat{y}_{t+h-1}$ is appended to the historical state vector, and lag features are re-computed dynamically without future lookahead.
- **Uncertainty Bounds:** Residual standard error propagated over horizon: $\hat{y}_{t+h} \pm 1.96 \cdot \hat{\sigma}_\varepsilon \sqrt{h}$.

### Model 2: ARIMA (Auto p, d, q)
- **Specification:** AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average $\text{ARIMA}(p, d, q)$:
  $$\phi(B)(1 - B)^d y_t = \theta(B)\varepsilon_t$$
- **Parameter Selection:** Automatic bounded candidate search over $p \in [0, 3]$, $d \in [0, 2]$, $q \in [0, 3]$. Optimal parameters are chosen strictly via Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC).
- **Multi-Step Strategy:** Analytical state-space forecast via Statsmodels `get_forecast(steps=horizon)` with exact asymptotic 95% confidence intervals.

### Model 3: SARIMA (Seasonal ARIMA)
- **Specification:** Seasonal AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average:
  $$\Phi_P(B^s)\phi_p(B)(1 - B)^d (1 - B^s)^D y_t = \Theta_Q(B^s)\theta_q(B)\varepsilon_t$$
- **Seasonal Period:** $s = 5$ observations (business trading week).
- **Diagnostics:** AIC/BIC penalized comparison verifies whether seasonal parameters improve goodness-of-fit over standard ARIMA without overfitting.

### Model 4: Holt's Exponential Smoothing
- **Specification:** Holt's Linear Trend method with additive damped trend option:
  $$\ell_t = \alpha y_t + (1 - \alpha)(\ell_{t-1} + \phi b_{t-1})$$
  $$b_t = \beta(\ell_t - \ell_{t-1}) + (1 - \beta)\phi b_{t-1}$$
  $$\hat{y}_{t+h|t} = \ell_t + \sum_{i=1}^h \phi^i b_t$$
  where $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ is the level smoothing parameter, $\beta \in (0, 1)$ is the trend smoothing parameter, and $\phi \in (0, 1]$ is the damping factor.
- **Model Selection:** Compares standard linear trend ($\phi = 1$) against damped linear trend ($\phi < 1$) using Sum of Squared Errors (SSE) on the training set.

### Model 5: Random Forest Regressor
- **Architecture:** Nonlinear ensemble of 150 randomized decision tree estimators with `max_depth = 8`, `min_samples_split = 5`, `min_samples_leaf = 2`.
- **Feature Pipeline:** Lags (1, 2, 3, 5, 10), rolling window statistics (mean 5, 10; std 5), and lagged percentage returns ($R_{t-1}$).
- **Multi-Step Strategy:** Recursive multi-step forecasting with dynamic feature vector reconstruction.
- **Uncertainty Bounds:** Empirical 10th and 90th percentiles aggregated across all individual decision trees in the ensemble.

### Benchmark: Naive Last-Observation Benchmark
- **Specification:**
  $$\hat{y}_{t+h|t} = y_t \quad \forall h \ge 1$$
- **Role:** Represents the Martingale hypothesis / Efficient Market Hypothesis baseline. Models must demonstrate empirical error reductions below the Naive Benchmark to be deemed predictive.

## 4. Evaluation and Backtesting Methodology

### 4.1 Chronological Split (No Data Leakage)
Standard random k-fold cross-validation causes severe future-data leakage in time series. FX Insight enforces strictly chronological training, validation, and testing partitions:
- Training: Initial 70% of chronological observations.
- Holdout / Test: Consecutive future observations.

### 4.2 Rolling-Origin Backtesting
Rolling-origin evaluation dynamically advances the forecast origin $t_0$ forward across multiple test slices:
1. Fit models on history available at $t_0$.
2. Generate multi-step forecasts for $h = 1 \dots H$.
3. Compute forecast errors against true realizations.
4. Advance origin $t_0 \leftarrow t_0 + \text{stride}$.
5. Aggregate out-of-sample error metrics across all evaluation origins.

### 4.3 Standardized Evaluation Metrics
Let $y_t$ denote the actual observation and $\hat{y}_t$ denote the model prediction for $t = 1 \dots n$:

1. **Mean Absolute Error (MAE):**
   $$\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n |y_t - \hat{y}_t|$$

2. **Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE):**
   $$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n (y_t - \hat{y}_t)^2}$$

3. **Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE %):**
   $$\text{MAPE} = \frac{100\%}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n \left| \frac{y_t - \hat{y}_t}{y_t} \right|$$

4. **Symmetric Mean Absolute Percentage Error (sMAPE %):**
   $$\text{sMAPE} = \frac{100\%}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n \frac{|y_t - \hat{y}_t|}{(|y_t| + |\hat{y}_t|) / 2}$$

5. **Directional Accuracy (DA %):**
   $$\text{DA} = \frac{100\%}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n \mathbb{I}\left( \text{sign}(y_t - y_{t-1}) == \text{sign}(\hat{y}_t - y_{t-1}) \right)$$
   Measures the percentage of periods in which the model correctly anticipated whether the currency pair appreciated or depreciated.
